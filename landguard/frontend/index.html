What is LANDGUARD?

A short product briefing.

The problem

Land records in Bangladesh live in many places — deeds, mutations, land-tax rolls, and Khatian registers — maintained by different authorities, often in isolation. Discrepancies between these records are a major source of fraud, double transfers, and contested ownership.

The solution

LANDGUARD treats the parcel as the central entity and collects evidence from every relevant record. A deterministic rule engine compares them, producing explainable findings. Every verification event is sealed into a tamper-evident audit chain.

What LANDGUARD does — and does not — claim

The wording is intentional.

Reports

  • Potential inconsistencies between records.
  • Missing evidence (e.g. no mutation for a transfer).
  • Owner, area, and document-hash mismatches.
  • Duplicate or overflowing active transfers.
  • Tampering of the audit chain.

Does not claim

  • A legal determination of who owns the land.
  • That a transaction is legally fraudulent.
  • Real cadastral surveying of physical boundaries.
  • Live access to government databases.
  • AI/ML adjudication of risk.

About this prototype

LANDGUARD is a student hackathon prototype. The records shown in the demo are synthetic: synthetic Bangladesh-style parcel identifiers, synthetic owner references (DEMO-ID-NNNN), synthetic deed numbers, synthetic geometry, and simulated adapters. The shape of the system is the deliverable — not the dataset.

The audit chain is real cryptographic code (SHA-256, deterministic canonicalization, hash linkage), and the verification engine is real deterministic logic — only the inputs are synthetic.
